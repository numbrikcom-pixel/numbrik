How to use the Z-Score Calculator

  1. Enter the raw score (x).
  2. Enter the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of the data.
  3. Read the z-score, the percentile and the tail probabilities.

How it works

A z-score says how many standard deviations a value is from the mean. A z-score of 0 is exactly average, +1 is one standard deviation above, and −2 is two below. Because it removes the units, you can compare scores from different tests, such as an SAT score and an ACT score.

If the data follow a normal distribution, the z-score converts to a percentile: about 68% of values fall within ±1, 95% within ±1.96 and 99.7% within ±3. The calculator uses the standard normal cumulative distribution to find the area to the left of your z-score.

Formula

z = (x − μ) ÷ σ
Percentile = Φ(z) × 100
Two-tailed p = 2 × (1 − Φ(|z|))

Example

A test has a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 10. A score of 85 has z = (85 − 70) ÷ 10 = 1.5. That's higher than about 93.32% of test takers (the 93rd percentile).

Frequently asked questions

What is a good z-score?

It depends on the context. Above +1 is better than about 84% of values, above +2 better than about 98%. In quality control, a z-score beyond ±3 usually flags an outlier.

Can a z-score be negative?

Yes. A negative z-score means the value is below the mean. z = −1 is lower than about 84% of values (the 16th percentile).

What's the z-score for 95% confidence?

1.96 for a two-tailed interval and 1.645 for one-tailed.

How do I convert a z-score to a percentile?

Find the area under the normal curve to the left of z. This calculator does it for you; z = 1 is the 84th percentile and z = 2 is the 98th.
