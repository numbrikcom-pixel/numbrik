How to use the Roman Numeral Converter

  1. Type a number (1 to 3,999), Roman numerals, or a date like 11/08/2026.

  3. Read the result and the breakdown of each part.

How it works

Roman numerals use seven letters: I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500 and M = 1,000. You add letters from largest to smallest, so VIII = 5 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 8. When a smaller letter comes before a larger one, you subtract it: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400 and CM = 900.

Standard numerals never repeat a letter more than three times in a row, which is why 4 is IV, not IIII (though many clock faces use IIII). Only I, X and C are used for subtraction. Without extra symbols, the largest number is 3,999 (MMMCMXCIX).

Formula

I = 1   V = 5   X = 10   L = 50
C = 100   D = 500   M = 1000
IV = 4  IX = 9  XL = 40  XC = 90  CD = 400  CM = 900

Example

To write 2026: 2000 = MM, 20 = XX, 6 = VI, so 2026 = MMXXVI. To read MCMXCIV: M (1000) + CM (900) + XC (90) + IV (4) = 1994.

Frequently asked questions

What is 2026 in Roman numerals?

MMXXVI: MM (2000) + XX (20) + VI (6).

How do you write 4 in Roman numerals?

IV, meaning one less than five. Some clocks show IIII, but IV is the standard form.

Is there a zero in Roman numerals?

No. The Romans had no numeral for zero. Medieval writers sometimes used N (for nulla) instead.

How do I write a date in Roman numerals?

Convert the month, day and year separately and join them with dots or dashes. November 8, 2026 is XI.VIII.MMXXVI in month-day-year order.
