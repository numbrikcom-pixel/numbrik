RMD Calculator

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 13, 2026

Work out how much you must withdraw from a traditional IRA, 401(k) or 403(b) this year. Enter your birth year and last year's December 31 balance to get your RMD, using the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table, and a 10-year projection.

How do you calculate your RMD?

Your RMD is last year's December 31 balance divided by the distribution period for your age at the end of this year, from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table.

How to use the RMD Calculator

  1. Enter your year of birth.
  2. Enter the distribution year (this year).
  3. Enter the account balance on December 31 of last year: it's on your year-end statement.
  4. Read your RMD, the factor used and when it's due.

How it works

Your RMD is last year's December 31 balance divided by the distribution period for your age at the end of this year, from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. At 75 the factor is 24.6, so you withdraw about 4.07% of the balance. The factor shrinks every year, so the percentage rises.

Since the SECURE 2.0 Act, RMDs start at 73 if you were born from 1951 to 1959, and at 75 if you were born in 1960 or later. Your first RMD can wait until April 1 of the following year, but then you take two in one year. After that, each RMD is due by December 31. Missing one triggers a 25% excise tax on the shortfall, cut to 10% if you correct it within two years. Roth IRAs have no RMDs for the original owner, and Roth 401(k)s haven't needed them since 2024.

Formula

RMD = balance on Dec 31 of the prior year ÷ distribution period
Distribution period: IRS Uniform Lifetime Table, by age at year end

Example

You were born in 1952, so you turn 74 in 2026. Your IRA held $500,000 on December 31, 2025. The factor for age 74 is 25.5, so your 2026 RMD is 500,000 ÷ 25.5 = $19,608, due by December 31, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

At what age do I have to take an RMD?

73 if you were born between 1951 and 1959, and 75 if you were born in 1960 or later.

What happens if I miss an RMD?

The IRS charges a 25% excise tax on the amount you should have taken. It drops to 10% if you take the missed amount and file a corrected return within two years.

Do Roth IRAs have RMDs?

Not for the original owner. Since 2024, Roth 401(k) and 403(b) accounts don't need RMDs during the owner's lifetime either. Inherited Roth accounts have their own rules.

Can I take my RMD from just one account?

For IRAs, yes: work out each IRA's RMD, then withdraw the total from any of them. 401(k) RMDs must come from each 401(k) separately.
