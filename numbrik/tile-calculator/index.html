Tile Calculator

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 8, 2026

Work out how many tiles you need for a floor, wall, shower or backsplash. Enter the area, your tile size and grout gap, and the calculator adds a waste allowance and tells you how many boxes to buy.

Loading calculator…

How to use the Tile Calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the area.
  2. Enter the tile size and grout joint.
  3. Set the waste allowance: 10% for straight layouts, 15% for diagonal or herringbone.
  4. Enter the tiles per box to get the number of boxes.

How it works

The calculator divides the area by the space one tile covers, including half a grout joint on each side, and rounds up. It then adds the waste allowance for cuts, breakages and mistakes.

Buy all your tile at once from the same batch (lot number), because shades vary between batches. Keep a few spare tiles after the job for future repairs. For rooms with lots of corners or a feature pattern, use 15–20% waste.

Formula

Tile area = (length + grout) × (width + grout)
Tiles = room area ÷ tile area × (1 + waste ÷ 100)
Boxes = tiles ÷ tiles per box, rounded up

Example

A 12 × 10 ft floor (120 sq ft) with 12 × 24 in tiles: each tile covers 2 sq ft, so you need 60 tiles. With 10% waste that's 66 tiles, or 9 boxes of 8. With 1/8-inch grout joints each tile covers slightly more floor, so the calculator shows 65.

Frequently asked questions

How many 12x24 tiles do I need for 100 square feet?

Each 12 × 24 tile covers 2 square feet, so 50 tiles, or 55 with 10% waste.

How much extra tile should I buy?

10% for a straight layout in a simple room, 15% for diagonal, herringbone or rooms with many cuts, and up to 20% for large-format tiles that break easily.

How do I measure an L-shaped room?

Split it into rectangles, work out each area, and add them together before entering the total.

Do I need to include grout lines?

For small tiles, yes. Grout joints add up: 1/8-inch joints on 4-inch tiles reduce the count by about 6%. For large tiles the difference is tiny.
