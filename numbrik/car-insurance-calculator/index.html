Car Insurance Calculator: Estimate Your Premium

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 21, 2026

How much is car insurance?

The US average for a 35-year-old with a clean record and good credit is about $2,357 a year for full coverage and $650 for minimum coverage in 2026. An at-fault accident adds about 49%, and a 20-year-old pays about twice as much.

How to use the Car Insurance Calculator

  1. Choose the United States or the United Kingdom.
  2. Pick your state and enter the driver's age.
  3. Choose full coverage (liability plus collision and comprehensive) or your state's minimum.
  4. Set your credit and your driving record for the last 3–5 years.
  5. Read your estimated premium, the monthly cost and the typical range of quotes.

How it works

The estimate starts from the average premium in your state for a 35-year-old driver with a clean record and good credit, from a November 2026 rate analysis. It then applies the average effect of each factor in that analysis. Age matters most for young drivers: a 20-year-old pays about twice as much as a 35-year-old, and prices ease slowly into your 60s before rising a little again. One speeding ticket adds about 28%, an at-fault accident about 49% and a DUI about 88% to a full-coverage premium.

Credit is a big factor in most states: drivers with poor credit pay about 67% more for full coverage than drivers with good credit, even with a clean record. California, Hawaii, Massachusetts and Michigan don't allow insurers to use credit for car insurance, so the calculator ignores it there.

Full coverage adds collision and comprehensive to liability, so it repairs or replaces your own car, and lenders require it on a financed or leased car. Minimum coverage is the legal minimum liability in your state; it's far cheaper but pays nothing toward your own car. Your real quote also depends on your car's make and model, your ZIP code, how far you drive and the insurer, which is why the result shows a ±20% range.

Formula

Estimate = state average × age factor × driving record factor × credit factor
Record (full coverage): ticket ×1.28, accident ×1.49, DUI ×1.88
Poor credit: ×1.67 (not used in CA, HI, MA, MI)

Example

A 35-year-old in Texas with a clean record and good credit pays about $3,640 a year for full coverage, or $303 a month. After one at-fault accident, that rises to about $5,427; at age 20 with a clean record, about $7,280.

Six ways to pay less

  1. Get at least three quotes at every renewal; prices for the same driver vary by hundreds of dollars between insurers.
  2. Raise your collision and comprehensive deductible from $500 to $1,000 if you can afford it after an accident.
  3. Bundle car and home or renters insurance with one company.
  4. Ask about good-driver, good-student, low-mileage and safety-course discounts.
  5. Drop collision and comprehensive on an old car worth less than about ten times the yearly cost of that cover.
  6. Keep your credit healthy, in states where insurers use it.

UK and Canada

In the UK, the average price paid for comprehensive car insurance was £562 a year in July–September 2026, according to the Association of British Insurers, which tracks about 28 million policies. Building a no-claims bonus and comparing quotes about three weeks before renewal usually lowers the price. In Canada, rules differ by province: British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba have public basic insurance, Quebec covers injuries through a public plan, and Ontario and Alberta have some of the highest private premiums.

Frequently asked questions

How much is car insurance per month?

About $196 a month for full coverage and $54 for minimum coverage for a 35-year-old with a clean record in 2026, but state matters a lot: from about $96 a month in Wyoming to $358 in Louisiana.

Why is car insurance so expensive for young drivers?

Drivers under 25 have far more crashes per mile driven, so insurers charge them more. A 20-year-old pays about twice what a 35-year-old pays; staying on a parent's policy is usually cheaper.

How much does an accident raise car insurance?

One at-fault accident raises a full-coverage premium by about 49% on average, and the surcharge usually lasts three to five years.

What's the difference between full and minimum coverage?

Minimum coverage is only the liability insurance your state requires, which pays for damage you cause to others. Full coverage adds collision and comprehensive, which pay to repair or replace your own car.

Is this calculator a quote?

No. It's an estimate from average rates. Your actual price depends on your car, ZIP code, mileage, claims history and the insurer, so compare quotes before you buy.
