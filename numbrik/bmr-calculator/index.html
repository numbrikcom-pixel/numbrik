BMR Calculator

Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to keep you alive: breathing, circulation, temperature and cell repair. Enter your sex, age, height and weight to see your BMR and the calories you burn each day at different activity levels.

How to use the BMR Calculator

  1. Choose male or female, and US units or metric.
  2. Enter your age, height and weight.
  3. Read your BMR from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, with the Harris-Benedict result for comparison, and your daily calories for each activity level.

How it works

The main result uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation from 1990. Studies comparing prediction equations with measured metabolic rate found it the most accurate for most adults, usually within 10% of the measured value. It uses weight in kilograms, height in centimetres and age in years, with a constant that differs for men and women.

BMR is only the energy you'd burn lying still all day. To estimate the calories you actually burn, called total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), the calculator multiplies BMR by an activity factor from 1.2 for a desk job with no exercise to 1.9 for hard daily training or a physical job.

Formula

Men:   BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161
TDEE = BMR × activity factor (1.2 to 1.9)

Example

A 35-year-old man who is 178 cm (5 ft 10 in) tall and weighs 82 kg (181 lb) has a BMR of 10 × 82 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 35 + 5 = 1,762 calories a day. The revised Harris-Benedict equation gives 1,842. If he is moderately active, his daily burn is about 2,732 calories.

A 30-year-old woman who is 165 cm tall and weighs 65 kg has a BMR of about 1,370 calories.

BMR vs TDEE vs RMR

BMR is measured lying still after a night's sleep and a 12-hour fast. RMR (resting metabolic rate) is measured under looser conditions and runs slightly higher. TDEE adds everything else you do in a day, from walking and exercise to digesting food. To lose weight you eat below your TDEE, not below your BMR.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal BMR?

Most adult women have a BMR between about 1,200 and 1,500 calories a day and most adult men between about 1,500 and 1,900. Taller, heavier and younger people burn more.

Should I eat less than my BMR to lose weight?

Generally no. Eating below your BMR for long periods can cost muscle and is hard to sustain. Aim for a moderate deficit below your TDEE instead, such as 300–500 calories a day.

Why does BMR go down with age?

Adults tend to lose muscle as they age, and muscle burns more energy than fat. Strength training helps keep it up.

How accurate is a BMR calculator?

For most people the Mifflin-St Jeor equation is within about 10% of a lab measurement. It is less accurate for very muscular people and people with obesity.
