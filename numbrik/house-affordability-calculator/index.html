How Much House Can I Afford?

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 13, 2026

Find a realistic home price for your budget. Enter your income, monthly debts, down payment and mortgage rate, and the calculator applies the debt-to-income rules lenders use, including property tax, insurance, HOA fees and PMI.

How much house can I afford?

Under the 28/36 rule, total housing costs should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income, and all debts no more than 36%. On a $100,000 salary that's about $2,333 a month, or roughly a $320,000 home with $40,000 down at 6.5%.

How to use the House Affordability Calculator

  1. Enter your yearly household income before tax.
  2. Add your monthly debt payments, such as car, student loan and credit card minimums.
  3. Enter your down payment, mortgage rate and term.
  4. Adjust property tax, insurance and HOA for the area you're looking in, then read your maximum home price.

How it works

Lenders look at two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios. The 28/36 rule says your housing costs (mortgage principal and interest, property tax, insurance, HOA and PMI) shouldn't be more than 28% of your gross monthly income, and all your debts together shouldn't be more than 36%. FHA loans and many lenders allow more, often around 31/43, and sometimes higher with strong credit or savings.

The calculator takes the lower of the two limits as your maximum monthly payment, then works backwards to the home price that payment supports. With less than 20% down it adds an estimate for private mortgage insurance (PMI). Just because a lender will approve an amount doesn't mean it's comfortable: leave room for repairs, savings and closing costs, which are typically 2–5% of the price.

Formula

Max housing payment = lower of
  28% × monthly income
  36% × monthly income − other debts
Home price = down payment + loan the payment supports
(after property tax, insurance, HOA and PMI)

Example

A household earning $100,000 a year with $500 of monthly debts and $40,000 down, at a 6.5% 30-year rate: 28% of $8,333 is $2,333 and 36% minus debts is $2,500, so the limit is $2,333 a month. After taxes, insurance and PMI that supports a home of about $320,000.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on a $100,000 salary?

Roughly $313,000 with 10% down, a 6.5% rate and $300 of other monthly debts, using the 28/36 rule. More debt or higher taxes lower that; a bigger down payment raises it.

What is the 28/36 rule?

Spend no more than 28% of your gross monthly income on housing and no more than 36% on all debts combined, including housing.

How much should I put down on a house?

20% avoids PMI on a conventional loan, but many buyers put down 3–10%. FHA loans need as little as 3.5% with a credit score of 580 or higher.

Does the calculator include closing costs?

No. Budget another 2–5% of the price for closing costs, plus moving costs and an emergency fund.
