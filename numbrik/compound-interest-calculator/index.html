How to use the Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Enter your starting amount and how much you'll add each month.
  2. Enter the yearly interest rate, the number of years and how often interest compounds.
  3. Read the final balance and check the table to see how interest takes over in later years.

How it works

With compound interest you earn interest on your interest. Each period the interest is added to the balance, so the next period's interest is a little larger. The calculator converts the yearly rate into a monthly rate for the chosen compounding, then adds each month's interest and deposit.

More frequent compounding earns slightly more. A 7% rate compounded monthly is an effective 7.23% a year. Real investments go up and down, so treat a fixed rate as a planning estimate. Taxes, fees and inflation are not included.

Formula

Without deposits:  A = P × (1 + r/k)^(k×t)
P = starting amount, r = yearly rate, k = compounding periods per year, t = years

Example

$10,000 at 7% compounded monthly, plus $200 a month for 10 years, grows to about $54,714. You put in $34,000, so about $20,714 is interest.

The rule of 72 gives a quick estimate: divide 72 by the interest rate to find roughly how many years it takes money to double. At 8%, that's about 9 years.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the original amount. Compound interest is also paid on interest already earned, so the balance grows faster each year.

Does monthly compounding make a big difference?

A little. At 7%, monthly compounding gives an effective 7.23% a year instead of 7%.

How long does it take to double my money?

Use the rule of 72: divide 72 by the yearly rate. At 6% it takes about 12 years.

Are taxes included?

No. Interest in a taxable account may be taxed each year, which lowers the final balance.
