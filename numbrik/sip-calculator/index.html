SIP Calculator

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 18, 2026

See what a monthly SIP (systematic investment plan) in a mutual fund could grow to. Enter your monthly amount, expected return and time period, and optionally raise the SIP each year, to see total invested, estimated returns and the final value.

How much will my SIP grow?

At 12% a year, ₹10,000 a month for 15 years grows to about ₹50,45,760 on ₹18,00,000 invested. The formula is FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i).

How to use the SIP Calculator

  1. Enter how much you'll invest each month.
  2. Enter the return you expect per year, such as 12% for an equity fund.
  3. Enter the number of years.
  4. Optionally add a yearly step-up, the percentage by which you'll raise your SIP each year.
  5. Read the estimated final value, the amount you invested and the year-by-year growth.

How it works

Each monthly instalment is invested and grows at the monthly rate (annual return ÷ 12) until the end of the period. Instalments made early compound for longer, which is why the gains grow faster in the later years. This calculator assumes each SIP is invested at the start of the month, the same convention most fund houses' calculators use.

A step-up SIP raises the monthly amount once a year, typically in line with your salary. Even a 10% yearly step-up makes a large difference over 15–20 years. Real mutual fund returns vary from year to year and can be negative; the result is an estimate, not a guarantee.

Formula

FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)
P = monthly SIP, i = annual return ÷ 12 ÷ 100, n = months
Returns = FV − total invested

Example

A ₹10,000 SIP for 15 years at 12% a year grows to about ₹50,45,760: ₹18,00,000 invested and ₹32,45,760 in returns. With a 10% yearly step-up, it grows to about ₹86,83,849 on ₹38,12,698 invested.

Frequently asked questions

How is SIP return calculated?

With FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is the monthly SIP, i is the monthly return and n the number of months. ₹10,000 a month for 15 years at 12% gives about ₹50.5 lakh.

What is a good SIP return?

Indian equity mutual funds have historically returned around 10–14% a year over long periods, but returns vary and past performance doesn't guarantee future results. Debt funds have typically returned 6–8%.

What is a step-up SIP?

A SIP where you raise the monthly amount each year by a fixed percentage or amount, usually as your income grows. It can greatly increase your final corpus.

Is SIP better than a lump sum?

A SIP spreads your investment over time, so you buy at different prices and don't need a large amount upfront. A lump sum invested early can earn more if markets rise, but carries more timing risk.
