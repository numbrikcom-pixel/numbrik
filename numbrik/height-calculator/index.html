How to use the Height Calculator

  1. Choose boy or girl and your units.
  2. Pick a method: parents' heights, or the child's height as a toddler.
  3. Enter the heights.
  4. Read the predicted adult height and the likely range.

How it works

The mid-parental height method averages the parents' heights and adjusts for sex: add 13 cm (5 inches) to the mother's height for a boy, or subtract 13 cm from the father's height for a girl, then average with the other parent. Paediatricians use it to see whether a child is growing along the expected path. About 95% of children end up within roughly 4 inches (10 cm) of the result.

The doubling rule is older and rougher: boys reach about twice their height at age 2, and girls about twice their height at 18 months. More accurate methods, such as Khamis-Roche, also use the child's current height and weight, and a bone-age X-ray is the most precise. Nutrition, sleep, health and puberty timing all play a part.

Formula

Boy = (father + mother + 13 cm) ÷ 2
Girl = (father + mother − 13 cm) ÷ 2
In inches: use 5 in instead of 13 cm
Likely range: ± 10 cm (about 4 in)

Example

Father 5 ft 10 in (70 in), mother 5 ft 5 in (65 in). A son is predicted at (70 + 65 + 5) ÷ 2 = 70 in (5 ft 10 in), likely between 5 ft 6 in and 6 ft 2 in. A daughter: (70 + 65 − 5) ÷ 2 = 65 in (5 ft 5 in).

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the height calculator?

The mid-parental method puts most children within about 4 inches (10 cm) of the prediction. It can't account for nutrition, health problems or unusually early or late puberty.

When do boys and girls stop growing?

Most girls reach adult height around 14 to 15, a couple of years after their first period. Most boys stop around 16 to 18, though some grow a little into their early 20s.

Can my child be taller than both parents?

Yes. Height comes from many genes, and better nutrition means many children outgrow their parents. The prediction is only the most likely outcome.

Is the doubling rule reliable?

It's a rough rule of thumb. It works best as a quick estimate; the parents' heights method is usually closer.
