Life Insurance Calculator: How Much Term Life Do You Need?

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 21, 2026

Work out how much term life insurance would protect your family if you died, and which term length fits. Add your income, debts and what your children will need, subtract what you already have, and see a coverage amount, a suggested term and, in the US, what that policy typically costs at your age.

How much life insurance do I need?

Add your income for the years your family would need it, your mortgage and debts, children's education and final costs, then subtract savings and existing cover. A $75,000 earner supporting a family for 15 years with a $220,000 mortgage often needs about $1.5 million.

How to use the Life Insurance Calculator

  1. Choose your currency: US, UK or Canadian dollars.
  2. Enter your yearly income and how many years your family would rely on it, usually until your youngest child is independent.
  3. Add your mortgage balance, other debts, college costs and final expenses.
  4. Subtract savings and any life insurance you already have, such as cover through work.
  5. In the US, add your age, sex and smoking status to see a typical monthly cost.

How it works

A life insurance payout has one job: to let the people who depend on you carry on without your income. This calculator uses a needs-based method, a version of what planners call the DIME method (Debt, Income, Mortgage, Education). It adds up your income for the years your family would need it, the mortgage and other debts they'd want to clear, education for each child and final expenses such as a funeral. It then subtracts savings, investments and existing life cover, and rounds up to the nearest $50,000, since policies are sold in round amounts.

The term should last as long as the need does. If your youngest child is 5 and you want cover until they're 22, a 20-year term fits; if your mortgage has 25 years left, consider 25 or 30. Term life is the simplest and cheapest kind: you pay a fixed premium for the term, and the policy pays out only if you die during it. Permanent policies (whole and universal life) cost several times more for the same cover.

The cost estimate uses average 2026 monthly rates for nonsmokers in good health, by age and sex, for $250,000, $500,000 and $1 million policies over 10, 20 and 30 years, and fills in amounts and ages in between. It shows a range of ±15% because insurers price your exact health class after a medical questionnaire or exam. Smoking multiplies the price about three times.

Formula

Need = income × years + mortgage + other debts + (children × college cost) + final expenses
Cover = need − savings − existing life insurance (rounded up to $50,000)
Term = shortest of 10/15/20/25/30 years that covers the years needed

Example

A 35-year-old earning $75,000 wants to support the family for 15 years, has a $220,000 mortgage, $15,000 of other debt, two children ($60,000 each for college) and $15,000 for final costs. That's $1,495,000. Minus $40,000 of savings and existing cover, the need is $1,455,000, so a $1,500,000, 15-year term policy fits. For a healthy nonsmoking man, a 20-year, $1 million policy averages about $65 a month at 35.

Quick rules of thumb, and why they fall short

  • 10× your income is a common shortcut. It ignores your mortgage, savings and how long your family needs support, so it can be far too much or too little.
  • Stay-at-home parents need cover too. Replacing childcare and household work can cost $30,000 or more a year; enter that as income.
  • Cover through work is usually 1–2× salary and ends if you leave the job, so count it, but don't rely on it alone.

In the UK and Canada

The coverage method works the same way: use the £ or C$ setting. In the UK, consider a decreasing term policy if the main need is a repayment mortgage, and ask for the policy to be written in trust so the payout can avoid inheritance tax and probate delays. In Canada, term life payouts to a named beneficiary are generally tax-free.

Frequently asked questions

How much life insurance do I need?

Enough to replace your income for the years your family depends on it, clear your mortgage and debts, fund education and pay final costs, minus your savings and existing cover. For a typical family with a $75,000 income that's often $1 million to $1.5 million.

How much does term life insurance cost?

A healthy 35-year-old nonsmoker pays about $29–$36 a month for a $500,000, 20-year policy in 2026. At 50 it's about $79–$103, and smokers pay about three times as much.

How long should my term be?

Long enough to cover your longest money commitment: until your youngest child finishes school or your mortgage is paid off. Most families choose 20 or 30 years.

Is term or whole life insurance better?

For most families, term. It costs a fraction of whole life for the same cover. Whole life adds lifelong cover and a cash value, which mainly suits estate planning or special needs.

Is a life insurance payout taxable?

In the US and Canada, a death benefit paid to a named beneficiary is generally not income tax. In the UK, it can count toward inheritance tax unless the policy is written in trust.
