Scientific Calculator

A free online calculator for everyday sums and scientific maths: trigonometry in degrees or radians, logarithms, powers, roots, percentages and factorials. Type on your keyboard or tap the keys.

Loading calculator…

How to use the Scientific Calculator

  1. Tap the keys or type with your keyboard. The answer previews as you type.
  2. Press = or Enter to finish. The last five answers appear in the result panel.
  3. Use Ans to reuse the previous answer, and switch between degrees and radians for sin, cos and tan.

How it works

The calculator follows the standard order of operations: brackets first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. So 2 + 3 × 4 is 14, not 20. Multiplication can be implied, so 2π and 3(4 + 1) work as you would write them.

Trigonometry uses degrees by default, the usual choice for school geometry. Switch to radians for calculus and physics. A minus sign written before a power applies after it, so −3² is −9, while (−3)² is 9. Brackets you forget to close are closed for you.

Example

sin(30) in degrees is 0.5. 2^10 is 1024, √(144) is 12, 5! is 120 and log(1000) is 3. To find 15% of 80, type 80 × 15% to get 12.

Frequently asked questions

Does it use the right order of operations?

Yes. Brackets come first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction, so 2 + 3 × 4 = 14.

How do I switch between degrees and radians?

Use the Degrees and Radians buttons above the keypad. The choice applies to sin, cos and tan.

Can I use my keyboard?

Yes. Type numbers and + − * / ^ ( ) %, press Enter for equals, Backspace to delete and Esc to clear.

Why does it say Error?

The expression can't be worked out, for example dividing by zero, the square root of a negative number, or an unfinished expression such as 5 ×.
