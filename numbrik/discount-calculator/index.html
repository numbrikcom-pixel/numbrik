Discount Calculator

How to use the Discount Calculator

  1. Enter the original (tag) price.
  2. Enter the discount, such as 25 for 25% off.
  3. Optional: add an extra discount and your sales tax rate.
  4. Read the sale price, your savings and the effective discount.

How it works

The sale price is the original price multiplied by (1 − discount ÷ 100). A $80 item at 25% off costs $80 × 0.75 = $60, so you save $20.

Stacked discounts multiply rather than add. “25% off, then an extra 10% off” takes 10% off the $60 sale price, not the original $80, giving $54. That's 32.5% off in total, not 35%. Sales tax is charged on the price after discounts in most US states.

Formula

Sale price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)
Stacked: price × (1 − d1 ÷ 100) × (1 − d2 ÷ 100)
You save = price − sale price

Example

A jacket tagged $80 is 25% off, with an extra 10% off at checkout. First discount: $80 × 0.75 = $60. Extra discount: $60 × 0.90 = $54. You save $26, which is 32.5% off.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the price by the discount percentage and divide by 100 to get the savings, then subtract it. 30% off $45 is $45 × 0.30 = $13.50 off, so you pay $31.50.

Is 20% off plus an extra 20% off the same as 40% off?

No. The second 20% comes off the already-reduced price, so the total is 36% off. On $100 you'd pay $64, not $60.

How do I find the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount). If something costs $60 after 25% off, the original price was $60 ÷ 0.75 = $80.

Is sales tax calculated before or after the discount?

For store discounts and coupons, sales tax is usually charged on the discounted price. Manufacturer coupons can be taxed on the full price in some states.
