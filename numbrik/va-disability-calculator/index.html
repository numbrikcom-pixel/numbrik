VA Disability Calculator

By the Numbrik team · Tested against worked examples · Updated November 18, 2026

Enter your individual VA disability ratings to see how VA combines them, the rating it rounds to, and your 2026 monthly compensation, including extra pay for a spouse and children. VA doesn't add ratings together, which is why 50% and 30% don't make 80%.

How is a combined VA disability rating calculated?

VA combines ratings highest first, each applied to the remaining able percentage, then rounds to the nearest 10. 50%, 30% and 10% make 69%, rounded to 70%, which pays $1,808.45 a month for a veteran alone in 2026.

How to use the VA Disability Calculator

  1. Type each of your service-connected ratings, separated by commas, such as 50, 30, 10.
  2. Choose whether you're married and, if so, whether your spouse needs Aid and Attendance.
  3. Enter how many children you have under 18 and how many aged 18 to 23 are in school.
  4. Read your combined rating, monthly and yearly pay, and the VA math step by step.

How it works

VA uses a "whole person" method. You start as 100% able. Your highest rating takes its percentage of that whole; each next rating then takes its percentage of what is left, not of 100%. VA rounds each step to a whole number, the same as its combined ratings table, and then rounds the final value to the nearest 10 (a value ending in 5 rounds up).

For ratings of 50%, 30% and 10%: 50% of 100 leaves 50; 30% of the remaining 50 is 15, so you're at 65; 10% of the remaining 35 is 3.5, giving 68.5, which the table shows as 69 and VA rounds to 70%. Monthly pay then comes from VA's rate table for that combined rating. At 30% and above, VA pays more for a spouse, children and dependent parents.

Formula

Remaining = 100
For each rating, highest first:
  combined = combined + remaining × rating ÷ 100
  remaining = 100 − combined
Final rating = combined rounded to the nearest 10

Example

Ratings of 50%, 30% and 10% combine to 69%, which VA rounds to 70%. A single veteran with no dependents at 70% receives $1,808.45 a month in 2026. Married with one child, the same veteran receives $2,074.45.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Bilateral factor: when both arms or both legs (or paired organs) are rated, VA adds 10% of their combined value before combining with other ratings, so your result may be slightly higher.
  • Dependent parents and special monthly compensation (SMC) for things like loss of use or housebound status are paid on top and aren't included.
  • A new cost-of-living adjustment takes effect each December 1. We update this page when VA publishes the new table.

Frequently asked questions

How does VA calculate combined disability ratings?

VA applies your ratings one at a time, highest first, each to the part of you that isn't already disabled. 50% and 30% make 65% (30% of the remaining 50 is 15), which rounds to 70%. The exact value is rounded to the nearest 10.

Why doesn't 50% plus 30% equal 80%?

Because VA doesn't add ratings. After a 50% rating, only 50% of you is considered able, so a 30% rating takes 30% of that 50, which is 15 points, for 65% combined, rounded to 70%.

How much is 100% VA disability in 2026?

A veteran alone at 100% receives $3,938.58 a month from December 1, 2025, about $47,263 a year, tax-free. With a spouse it's $4,158.17, and with a spouse and one child $4,318.99.

Is VA disability pay taxable?

No. VA disability compensation isn't taxed by the federal government or by states, and it doesn't count as income on your federal return.

When do dependents increase VA pay?

Only at a combined rating of 30% or higher. You need to add dependents with VA (for example on VA Form 21-686c) before the extra amount is paid.
