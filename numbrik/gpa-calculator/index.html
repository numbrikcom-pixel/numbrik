GPA Calculator

Add your courses, letter grades and credits to get your semester GPA on the 4.0 scale. Add your current GPA and credits to see your new cumulative GPA.

How to use the GPA Calculator

  1. Enter each course with its letter grade and credit hours. Use Add course for more rows.
  2. Remove example rows you don't need with ✕.
  3. To see your cumulative GPA, enter your current GPA and the credits you have already earned.
  4. To plan ahead, enter a target GPA and the credits you'll take next to see the GPA you need in those classes.

How it works

Each letter grade has a grade-point value. The calculator multiplies each grade's points by the course credits, adds them up and divides by the total credits, so a 4-credit course counts more than a 1-credit lab.

This is an unweighted GPA. A+ counts as 4.0, which is the most common practice, though some schools give 4.3. High schools that add points for honors or AP classes report a weighted GPA, which can go above 4.0. Pass/fail courses usually don't count toward GPA, so leave them out.

Formula

GPA = Σ(grade points × credits) ÷ Σ credits
Cumulative = (current GPA × earned credits + new grade points) ÷ (earned + new credits)

Example

The example term has five courses worth 14 credits and 50.4 grade points, a semester GPA of 3.60. A student who already had a 3.40 GPA over 45 credits would move to a cumulative 3.45.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good GPA?

A 3.0 is a B average. Many competitive programs and scholarships look for 3.5 or higher, but requirements differ by school.

How can I raise my GPA fastest?

Higher grades in courses with more credits move your GPA the most. The more credits you have already earned, the slower your cumulative GPA changes.

Does a withdrawn or pass/fail course count?

Usually not. A W or a pass grade normally carries no grade points, so leave it out. Check your school's policy.

Do Australian or UK universities use this scale?

Not usually. Most Australian universities use a 7-point GPA, and UK degrees use classifications such as First or 2:1. This calculator uses the US 4.0 scale.

What GPA do I need to reach my goal?

Enter your current GPA, credits earned, your target and the credits you'll take next. The calculator shows the GPA those new credits must average. For example, going from 3.0 over 60 credits to 3.2 with 30 more credits needs a 3.6.
