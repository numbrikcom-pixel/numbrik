How do you calculate a pay raise?

A percentage raise multiplies your pay by one plus the percentage: a 4% raise on $50,000 is $50,000 × 1.04 = $52,000.

How to use the Pay Raise Calculator

  1. Choose whether you know the raise percent, the raise amount, or your new pay.
  2. Enter your current pay and whether it's hourly or yearly.
  3. Read your new pay per hour, week, month and year, and how much more you'll earn.

How it works

A percentage raise multiplies your pay by one plus the percentage: a 4% raise on $50,000 is $50,000 × 1.04 = $52,000. A flat raise simply adds the amount. To find the raise percentage from old and new pay, divide the difference by the old pay.

Hourly and yearly figures assume 40 hours a week for 52 weeks (2,080 hours). The extra pay is before tax, so the increase in your paycheck will be smaller. Compare your raise with inflation: if prices rose 3% over the year, a 3% raise keeps your buying power level.

Formula

New pay = old pay × (1 + raise% ÷ 100)
Raise % = (new pay − old pay) ÷ old pay × 100
Yearly = hourly × 2,080

Example

A 5% raise on $24.00 an hour gives $25.20 an hour, which is $52,416 a year, or $2,496 more than before. Going from $50,000 to $53,000 is a raise of 3,000 ÷ 50,000 = 6%.

How big is a typical raise?

Surveys of US employers by consultancies such as WTW and Mercer have put typical salary increase budgets at around 3–4% in recent years. Promotions and job changes usually bring larger jumps, often 10% or more.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 3% raise?

Multiply your pay by 1.03. On $45,000 that's $46,350, an extra $1,350 a year.

How do I work out my raise percentage?

Subtract your old pay from your new pay, divide by your old pay and multiply by 100. From $20 to $21.50 an hour is a 7.5% raise.

Is a raise before or after tax?

Raises are quoted on gross pay, before tax. Your take-home increase will be smaller.

What's a good raise?

Anything above the inflation rate increases your buying power. Around 3–4% is common for a yearly raise; 10% or more is typical for a promotion.
