How it works

X% of Y multiplies Y by X and divides by 100. It also shows Y with that percentage added and taken away, which is handy for discounts and price rises.

X is what percent of Y divides X by Y and multiplies by 100.

Percent change divides the difference by the starting value. A change is not symmetrical: going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, but going from 100 back to 80 is a 20% decrease, because the starting value is different.

Formula

X% of Y          = X ÷ 100 × Y
X as % of Y      = X ÷ Y × 100
Percent change   = (new − old) ÷ |old| × 100

Example

15% of 240 is 36. 45 is 25% of 180. A price that moves from 80 to 100 has risen by +25%.

Everyday percentage examples

  • Sales: 25% off $80 is $20 off, so you pay $60.
  • Sales tax or VAT: adding 8% to $50 gives $54. To remove 20% VAT from a £120 price, divide by 1.20 to get £100.
  • Pay rise: a raise from $52,000 to $54,600 is a 5% increase.
  • Test scores: 42 out of 50 is 84%.
  • Investments: a fall from $1,000 to $850 is a 15% decrease, and you need a 17.6% gain to get back to $1,000.

Quick mental maths

To find 10%, move the decimal point one place left. 5% is half of that, 20% is double it, and 15% is 10% plus 5%. So 15% of $64 is $6.40 + $3.20 = $9.60.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the price by the discount rate. 30% off $70 is 0.30 × 70 = $21 off, so you pay $49.

How do I reverse a percentage increase?

Divide by 1 plus the rate. If a price is $120 after a 20% increase, the original was 120 ÷ 1.20 = $100.

What is the difference between percent change and percentage points?

Percentage points measure the gap between two percentages. A rate moving from 4% to 5% is a rise of 1 percentage point but a 25% increase.

Why doesn't +25% then −25% get back to the start?

The second change applies to a bigger number. 100 + 25% = 125, and 125 − 25% = 93.75.
