How to use the Take-Home Pay Calculator

  1. Choose single or married filing jointly.
  2. Enter your annual salary and how often you are paid.
  3. Add pre-tax deductions per year, such as 401(k) contributions, and an estimated state income tax rate.
  4. Read your take-home pay per paycheck and the breakdown of each tax.

How it works

Federal income tax is progressive: each slice of taxable income is taxed at its own rate. Taxable income is your salary minus pre-tax deductions and the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100 for single filers or $32,200 for married couples filing jointly.

Social Security tax is 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare tax is 1.45% of all wages, plus an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly). Traditional 401(k) contributions lower your income tax but not Social Security or Medicare, and the calculator treats all pre-tax deductions that way.

State tax here is a simple flat estimate. Real state taxes use their own brackets and deductions, and some cities, such as New York City, add a local income tax. Tax credits, extra W-4 withholding and the new deductions for tips and overtime are not included, so your actual paycheck can differ.

Formula

Taxable income = salary − pre-tax deductions − standard deduction
Federal tax    = sum of each bracket slice × its rate
Social Security = 6.2% × min(salary, $184,500)
Medicare       = 1.45% × salary (+0.9% above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint)
Take-home      = salary − pre-tax − federal − Social Security − Medicare − state

Example

A single filer earning $65,000 with no pre-tax deductions in a state with no income tax pays about $5,620 in federal income tax, $4,030 in Social Security and $942 in Medicare. Take-home pay is about $54,408 a year, or $2,092.60 every two weeks.

A married couple earning $100,000 together, putting $6,000 into a 401(k) and paying an estimated 5% state tax, takes home about $74,730 a year.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $60,000 a year after taxes?

For a single filer in a state with no income tax, about $50,390 a year, or $4,199 a month, in 2026. State and local taxes lower that figure.

What is my tax bracket?

Your bracket is the rate on your last dollar of taxable income, after the standard deduction. Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that rate, so your average (effective) rate is always lower than your top bracket.

Does a 401(k) reduce Social Security and Medicare tax?

No. Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal income tax and usually state tax, but Social Security and Medicare are still charged on that money.

Why is my real paycheck different?

Employers withhold based on your W-4, and paychecks can also include health insurance, local taxes, credits such as the child tax credit, and other deductions this estimate doesn't cover.
