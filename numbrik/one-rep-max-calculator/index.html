One Rep Max Calculator

Estimate the most weight you could lift for a single rep (your one rep max, or 1RM) from a set you've already done, without having to test a true max. Works for bench press, squat, deadlift and other lifts.

Loading calculator…

How to use the One Rep Max Calculator

  1. Enter the weight you lifted.
  2. Enter how many clean reps you did, from 1 to 12.
  3. Read your estimated 1RM and the training weights for each percentage of it.

How it works

The main estimate uses the Epley formula, the one most gym apps use. The Brzycki and Lombardi formulas are shown alongside so you can see the spread. All of them are most accurate between 2 and 10 reps. Above about 12 reps, endurance plays a bigger part and the estimates become unreliable.

Use a set taken close to failure with good form. The percentage chart turns your 1RM into training weights: strength programmes often work at 80–90% for 3–6 reps, and muscle-building programmes at 65–80% for 8–12 reps.

Formula

Epley:    1RM = w × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
Brzycki:  1RM = w × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)
Lombardi: 1RM = w × reps^0.10

Example

Bench pressing 225 lb for 5 reps gives an Epley estimate of 225 × (1 + 5/30) = 262.5 lb. Brzycki gives 253.1 lb and Lombardi 264.3 lb. For sets of 8, about 80% of the Epley estimate, or roughly 210 lb, is a sensible working weight.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a 1RM calculator?

Usually within a few percent for sets of 2–10 reps. Accuracy drops with higher reps and varies between people and lifts.

Which formula is best?

Epley and Brzycki give almost the same answer at around 10 reps. Epley reads a little higher at low reps. Take the average if you're unsure.

Is it safe to test a true one rep max?

It can be with good technique, a proper warm-up and a spotter or safety bars. Estimating from a set of 3–5 reps is safer for most people.

Can I use it for any exercise?

It works best for big barbell lifts such as bench press, squat, deadlift and overhead press.
